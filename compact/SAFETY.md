# ✦ The Warden's Compact ✦

*A Safety Framework for the Velvet Grimoire*

> *"Before the candle is lit, before the page is turned, before the first roll clatters upon the table — speak the compact. What is binding between us here, and what is barred."*

---

## I. Preamble — Why the Compact Exists

The Velvet Grimoire is a vessel for play that runs close to the bone. Its mechanic — **Fight, Flight, Freeze** — is borrowed from the body's oldest language, the one the nervous system speaks when the mind has run out of words. To play here with adult themes, with dark narrative, with intimacy and danger braided together, is to invite that language into the room on purpose.

That is a powerful thing. It can be a healing thing. It can also cut.

The Compact is the set of wards we raise around the table before any scene opens. It is not bureaucracy. It is not a mood-killer. It is the reason the scene can go *deeper* than it otherwise could — because both players know that the exits are marked, the ground rules are set, and nothing that happens inside the scene will be permitted to follow anyone out of it without consent.

Every rule below exists so that the scene can be bolder, not smaller.

---

## II. The Ember — The Pause Mechanic

At every moment of play, in every scene, on every screen, **there is a lit ember**. Either player may snuff it.

When the ember is snuffed, the game stops. Not slows. Stops.

- The narrative freezes. No one adds to it.
- The dice are set aside.
- The Grimoire's page stays open to the current scene — no advancing.
- The players step out of character. First names. Present tense. Grounded bodies.

**Three tiers of the Ember:**

| Signal | In-world name | What it means | What the other player does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🟡 Dim the Flame | *"Slow the page."* | Keep going, but softer. Ease intensity. Back off this particular element. | Adjusts tone. Pulls back on the specific thing. Does not ask why. Does not demand detail. |
| 🟠 Hold the Flame | *"Stay the page."* | Pause. I need a breath. I don't know yet if I want to continue. | Stops narration. Takes the next move off the player. Waits. Offers water, silence, or a hand — nothing else unless asked. |
| 🔴 Snuff the Ember | *"Close the book."* | End the scene. Now. No negotiation. | Closes the current scene. No "but we almost…", no "one more beat." Transition to Aftercare (§V). |

The Ember is one-sided and unconditional. Either player may signal any tier at any time for any reason or no reason. The Ember is never questioned in the moment. Reasons, if offered at all, come later and only if the signaling player chooses.

**Engine hook:** a persistent button/gesture must be present in both the DM admin and the NPC player view. Proposed: a candle glyph 🕯️ in a fixed corner, always visible, always clickable. Clicking opens the pause surface.

## III. The Threshold — The Pre-Session Rite

*No scene begins without the Threshold.* The landing page already greets players with: *"You stand at the threshold."* The Compact binds that moment into an actual ritual rather than a flavor line.

Before the first scene loads, both players speak to six questions. Silently to themselves; aloud to each other if they choose. The engine should surface these as a short form per session.

1. **Body.** What is my body reporting right now? (1–10 tension, hunger, pain, arousal, exhaustion.)
2. **Bandwidth.** On a scale of *candle-stub* to *bonfire*, how much am I bringing tonight?
3. **Green.** What am I actively hungry for in this scene?
4. **Yellow.** What am I open to but uncertain about? (Needs check-in mid-scene before crossing.)
5. **Red.** What is not welcome in this session — even if normally permitted? (Today's specific "not tonight" items, distinct from the permanent Banished Names in §VI.)
6. **Aftercare.** What do I want after the scene closes? (Quiet, food, touch, solitude, conversation, nothing yet.)

Both players' answers are visible to each other on the Threshold page before play begins. The DM updates the scene queue accordingly. No "surprise" content that violates a stated Yellow or Red. No exceptions.

**Duration:** 3 to 8 minutes. Longer is fine; shorter is suspicious.

## IV. The Banished Names and the Consecrated Pages

Every campaign carries two standing lists that transcend any single session.

### The Banished Names (Hard Limits)

Things that are **never** in a scene, ever. Not off-screen, not implied, not metaphorical. These are spoken once at campaign creation and stored in the Grimoire. Any scene tag matching a Banished Name is refused by the engine before the scene can launch.

Engine hook: the protagonist's existing *"Notes / Limits"* field is upgraded into a structured list. Each entry becomes a tag on the Banished Names roster. When a scene is saved with a matching tag, the admin surfaces a **red halt** and the scene cannot be queued for that protagonist until either the tag is removed or the protagonist retires.

### The Consecrated Pages (Lines and Veils)

Two gentler categories for content that may exist in the world but not on the page:

- **Hallowed Ground** — content that exists in the campaign's lore but is never narrated on screen. Referenced obliquely or after-the-fact at most.
- **Veiled Ground** — content that may be narrated, but briefly, impressionistically, with the camera turned away. "The door closes. Later, you remember only the smell of iron." No explicit detail.

Scenes are tagged at authorship with `hallowed`, `veiled`, or `open`. The Grimoire displays the tag prominently before the scene opens so both players know the register they are entering.

## V. The Hearth — Aftercare

*"The scene has closed. The book is shut. But the fire still warms the stones."*

Aftercare is not an extra. It is part of the session. A session that ends on the final scene's last die roll is a session that was never finished.

The Hearth is a structured wind-down. The engine should present it automatically upon session completion — or immediately, unconditionally, upon a **Snuff the Ember** signal.

**Seven Stations of the Hearth:**

1. **Breath.** Four slow rounds. Four in, six out. No counting aloud required; a visual guide on the page is enough.
2. **Body check.** Same 1–10 prompts as the Threshold. Compare. Notice what moved.
3. **Water.** Both players drink. The page will nag.
4. **Name the room.** Briefly: "I am [name]. The year is [year]. The room I am in is [room]. The person across from me is [partner's name]."
5. **Two words each.** Each player offers two words — no more, no fewer — for what the scene left them with. *Dazed. Warm. Rattled. Wanting. Quiet. Seen.* No discussion yet.
6. **Offer.** Each player offers what they have the capacity for right now: *hold me, leave me be, talk, eat, sleep.* The other accepts, declines, or counter-offers.
7. **Seal.** Close the Grimoire with intention. A breath, a bow, a joke, a kiss — whatever the pair has chosen as their ritual closer. The game is over when the seal is made, not before.

The Hearth takes as long as it takes. Minimum floor: five minutes, even if both players feel fine. "Feeling fine" arrives late when the session was heavy.

## VI. Content Wards on Scenes

Every scene authored in the Grimoire must carry at least one ward tag before it can be saved. The tag declares what is in the scene, so consent can be informed rather than ambushed.

Wards fall in three families:

- **Element wards** — what kind of content is present: `intimacy`, `violence`, `power-exchange`, `humiliation`, `restraint`, `blood`, `substance`, `surveillance`, `captivity`, etc. (The campaign begins with the canonical set below; both players may add names to the roster by mutual consent.)
- **Intensity wards** — how present: `suggested`, `explicit`, `visceral`. Determines register of narration.
- **Frame wards** — how framed: `consensual-in-fiction`, `non-consensual-in-fiction`, `dubious-consent-in-fiction`. (Fiction only; real-world consent is governed by the Compact, not the scene tag.)

The player view must display the full ward stack at the top of each scene *before* narration begins. The player confirms ("✦ Enter the Scene") or signals the Ember. No scene auto-advances past ward display without affirmative click.

**Canonical opening roster** (extend in campaign settings):
`intimacy · violence · power-exchange · restraint · humiliation · fear · blood · weaponry · substance · surveillance · captivity · religious-imagery · body-modification · interrogation · betrayal`

## VII. The Fourth Response

The Grimoire offers **Fight, Flight, Fawn, and Freeze** as the four branches of every scene. The autonomic nervous system recognizes Fawn as appeasement, compliance, or the self that survives by pleasing the threat.

For trauma-aware play, Fawn must be treated as a real response, not as a weaker form of Flight. A player whose dominant response is fawn should have a button that represents negotiation, tactical yielding, charm, de-escalation, or survival through agreement.

Every Fawn branch should be authored with the same weight as Fight, Flight, and Freeze. It should create consequence, information, leverage, or movement rather than merely rewarding compliance.

---

## VIII. The Warden's Watches — Mid-Scene Signals

The Ember is the big hammer. The Watches are the finer instruments for in-scene communication that doesn't require stopping play.

- **Dim** (🟡) — *This particular beat is too bright. Pull back on it without breaking scene.* Spoken or typed in the player scratchpad as `:dim:`.
- **Stoke** (🔥) — *This is working. Go further.* Spoken or `:stoke:`. Optional, helps the DM calibrate.
- **Veil** (🌫️) — *Keep going, but turn the camera away from this.* Narration becomes impressionistic for this beat.
- **Rewrite** (✍️) — *That last beat didn't land. Take it back. Re-narrate.* The DM strikes the last beat from the record and offers a softer one.

These are in-scene tools, not Ember-level escalations. They preserve flow while giving both players continuous steering.

## IX. Pacing and the Session Envelope

- **First session of any new campaign:** no `explicit` or `visceral` scenes. Build the world. Establish the NPC. End the session somewhere warm.
- **Any single session:** at most one `visceral` scene. Ending a session on a visceral scene is strongly discouraged — end on something that invites breath.
- **Between sessions involving heavy content:** at least one "quiet" session or one week, whichever the players prefer. The Grimoire should log this and surface a gentle warning when violated.
- **When life is bad outside the Grimoire:** play smaller. The page can tell — don't push it.

## X. Division of Wardship — Who Watches What

Because this is two-player with asymmetric roles, the watches are asymmetric too.

### The DM's Wardship
- Upholding the Banished Names against the scene roster.
- Reading Threshold state before launching the first scene.
- Surfacing ward tags before narration.
- Honoring Dim/Veil/Rewrite signals instantly.
- Calling the Hearth at session end, even when the NPC doesn't.
- Watching for *performed consent* — enthusiasm that sounds scripted, checking in when it does.

### The NPC Player's Wardship
- Honest Threshold answers, even when they would rather be up for it than not be.
- Naming Dim/Stoke/Veil signals as they arise, without self-censoring for the DM's sake.
- Using the Ember without apology or justification.
- Honoring the Hearth — not skipping, even when the scene "wasn't that heavy."

### Both Players' Wardship
- Speaking Yellow items before they become Red.
- Noticing when the other seems to have left the room — dissociation, going still, going performative — and calling a Hold.
- Keeping the out-of-game relationship out of the scene, and keeping the scene out of the out-of-game relationship unless explicitly invited.

## XI. When the Grimoire is the Wrong Book

The Velvet Grimoire is a game. It is not a therapist. It is not a replacement for a therapist. It is not a substitute for the kinds of support that trauma sometimes requires.

Use other books when:

- A scene activates material that the game cannot hold. The Ember is the first response; a clinician is the next.
- The Grimoire is becoming the only place where certain feelings are processed. Play is a tool, not the tool.
- A session leaves either player dysregulated for more than a day. Log the scene, talk to someone qualified, consider pausing the campaign.
- Fantasy and reality begin to blur in a way that scares either player.

Resources should live on the Hearth page and be reachable from the Ember surface. At minimum: a crisis line for the player's locale, a link to a grounding technique card, and a link to a therapist-finder for trauma-informed care.

---

## XII. The Bosses Ahead — When Other Players Enter

The current pair is DM + NPC. The roadmap adds **Playable Dungeon Bosses** — additional players taking antagonist roles.

The Compact extends, with two amendments:

1. **Every new player speaks the Compact before their first scene.** No exceptions, no "they know the drill." Fresh voices read the Ember section aloud.
2. **Banished Names are additive across the table.** Any player's Banished Name becomes the table's Banished Name for as long as they are seated. The engine enforces the union, not the intersection.

Additional wardship for Boss players:
- Boss scenes often carry the heaviest wards. The DM pre-clears scene tags with the protagonist's player before the Boss is given their cue.
- Bosses receive their NPC cues privately, as is already the engine's design, but the protagonist's Banished Names are surfaced to them **without disclosure of which names belong to which Compact** — they just see the consolidated list.

## XIII. The Seal

> *"What is bound here is bound well.*
> *What is barred here is barred wholly.*
> *The ember is lit. The book is open.*
> *When the hour turns, the ember may be snuffed by either hand,*
> *and no grievance, no shame, no question shall follow."*

Sign both names. Begin.
